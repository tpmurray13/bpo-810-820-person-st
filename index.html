Methodology and verification basis

No arm's-length sale of the assembled subject property is on record, and the client did not supply any marketing brochure or third-party comp package for this assignment. This BPO relies entirely on the sales comparison approach, built on comparable sales independently researched directly from county records.

  • Source of record for comps: the Cumberland County CAMA/tax property system and GIS Tax Parcels layer, queried directly for closed sales in the subject's own "King Street Area" assessor neighborhood.
  • Every comp below was pulled from a structured parcel-sales query and cross-checked against its own CAMA building detail record for year built, quality, and depreciation — none were taken from a listing site or brochure.
  • Per client instruction, this report also cites Grant-Murray's two most recent prior BPOs in this same corridor — 135 S King St ($800,000 recommended list price, a 57,964 SF industrial site with an as-is tire-stockpile encumbrance) and 110 S Racepath St ($455,000 recommended list price, a largely land-driven HI-zoned parcel) — for corridor context and as a cross-check on land value. Neither is used as a direct improved-building comp, since both differ materially in scale and condition from the subject; see Nearby prior opinions below.
  • Adjustments applied to each comp reflect Grant-Murray Real Estate's professional judgment as the prospective listing broker, including the ownership's specific direction to account for the subject's building size, its continuously maintained/operating condition, and the presence of an active business that will need to relocate. Where county assessment data runs counter to that direction, it is disclosed rather than omitted — see the adjustment matrix notes below.

Value analysis

Sales comparison approach (primary) — building. Grant-Murray gives primary weight to 138 N Racepath St, 312 N Racepath St, and 408 S Racepath St, the three closed sales closest to the subject in building size (3,200–6,520 SF) and recency (2023–2024). Their adjusted $/SF averages $52.52/SF. Applied to the subject's 5,984 SF county-recorded building area, this indicates a building value of $314,300. 912 Grove St and 107 S Broad St (large buildings, low $/SF) and 801 Person St (small building, high $/SF) bracket this figure but carry less weight given their scale mismatch with the subject.

Land add-on — ancillary parcels. Beyond the 0.22-acre parcel underlying the building, the subject includes 0.47 acres of additional LI-zoned land (802 Person St, 0.07 ac, and the unaddressed 0.40-ac parcel), used as yard/support space for the contractor operation. Using the land-value range independently established in Grant-Murray's own 110 S Racepath St BPO for similarly modest-improvement HI/LI land in this corridor ($100,000–$250,000/acre, with the strongest single comp at $300,000/acre), and applying a conservative rate toward the lower-middle of that range to reflect the LI zoning and support-yard function of these two parcels, $130,000/acre × 0.47 ac = $61,100 is added.

Combined sales comparison indicated value: $314,300 + $61,100 = $375,400.

Broker overlay — size, condition, and operating-business factors. Per ownership's explicit direction, Grant-Murray applies a final professional-judgment overlay of approximately +6.5% above the raw comp-derived figure, distinct from and in addition to the comp-level adjustments above, to reflect (1) the subject's functional scale as a complete, self-contained facility for an active trade/contractor operation, (2) its continuously maintained, air-conditioned, recently remodeled condition despite the county's 63% depreciation grade, and (3) the demonstrated commercial viability of an established, licensed business having operated there continuously — a factor the county's vacant-land-oriented comps at Racepath St do not share. This overlay is disclosed explicitly as broker judgment, not as additional comp-level evidence.

Reference only: the combined 2026 assessed value is $185,537, well below this indicated range — expected, since Cumberland County assessed values are known to lag verified market transactions, and since two of the three parcels' most recent deeds were non-arm's-length internal transfers rather than market sales.

Business relocation — practical disclosure

Arnold Service Company (McGowan Heating & Air, Inc.) is an active, ongoing HVAC contracting business operating from 820 Person St. Any sale to a third party will require the business to relocate its office, warehouse, service trucks, and inventory — a real transaction-timing consideration that a buyer will factor into negotiations (e.g., a post-closing leaseback or a delayed-possession closing date is common in owner-user sales of this type). This report values the fee-simple real estate on an as-if-delivered-vacant basis; it does not include any value attributable to the business itself (goodwill, equipment, customer list, or the HVAC license), which are personal property and intangible assets outside the scope of this real estate opinion.

Consistent with ownership's direction, the continuous operating history is treated as a positive indicator of the building's functional viability (see the Condition/use factors in the adjustment matrix above) rather than a value detractor — but the practical need to coordinate a relocation timeline should be disclosed to any prospective buyer and reflected in listing terms (e.g., marketing period, possession date, or a short-term leaseback).
